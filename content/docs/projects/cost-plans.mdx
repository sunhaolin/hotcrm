# Cost Plans

A **cost plan** (成本计划, `crm_cost_plan`) is one version of what a project is expected to cost, month by month. A presales project carries one as its **Bizcase**; a delivery project gets its first version by importing that Bizcase, and every later change to the budget is a new version. The plan is where the four cost categories are actually planned — the project records only read its totals.

## What a cost plan stores

| Group | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Plan Information** | Plan Number, Name, Cost Manager, the Presales Project *or* the Delivery Project it belongs to, Phase (*Bizcase / Delivery*), Version Number, Current Version, the plan it was cloned from, the budget adjustment that produced it |
| **Plan Amounts** | Frozen Baseline, Planned Total, and the four category totals — Labor Service, Third-party Service, Hardware/Software Procurement, Project Expense — plus *of which Travel* |
| **Comparison with the Current Version** | The version in force when the plan was submitted, its amounts, and the difference against each of this version's — see [What the approver sees](#what-the-approver-sees) |
| **Approval** | Approval Status *(read-only)*, Approved Date |

A plan belongs to exactly one project: a Bizcase hangs off a presales project, a delivery plan off a delivery project, and the phase is set from whichever you picked. Every amount on the plan is a **rollup of the month ledger** described below; none is typed in.

## Four line families, one per cost category

The requirement's four configuration screens are four objects, because the four categories are priced from different sources:

| Line | What you enter | Where the price comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Labor cost line** (人工服务成本行, `crm_labor_cost_line`) | Grade, headcount, hours per person per month, start and end month | The [rate card](/docs/projects/master-data): the hourly rate is copied onto the line and re-resolved for each month, so a card that changes mid-project prices the later months at the new rate |
| **Service cost line** (第三方服务成本行, `crm_service_cost_line`) | Service name, vendor, pricing basis (*per person-month, per person-day, lump sum*), unit price, headcount, duration | The supplier's quote, typed in; attach the quote to the plan |
| **Procurement cost line** (软硬件采购成本行, `crm_procurement_cost_line`) | Category (*hardware, software license, cloud service, maintenance*), optional product, quantity, unit price, delivery month and an optional amortisation end month | The product's list price when a product is picked, otherwise typed in |
| **Expense cost line** (项目费用成本行, `crm_expense_cost_line`) | Expense type; for **travel**: a travel standard, trips, travellers per trip, days per trip; for every **reimbursement** type (meetings, training, office, communications, entertainment, other): a budget amount | Travel is computed from the [travel standard](/docs/projects/master-data) — trips × travellers × (return fare + days × daily standard); reimbursements are budgeted directly |

Each line carries a description, a month range and a planned amount. The planned amount is the sum of the line's months, so the line and its split can never disagree.

## The month ledger

Saving a line generates its **cost plan months** (月度分解行, `crm_cost_plan_month`): one row per month in the line's range, each with the month, the category, the expense type for an expense line, the factors used and the amount.

- A labor month is headcount × hours × the rate effective in that month.
- A per-person-month service is unit price × headcount in each of its `duration` months; a per-person-day or lump-sum service, a procurement term and any expense line are spread evenly, with the last month taking the rounding remainder. A one-off purchase with no end month lands in its delivery month.
- Edit a month's amount and it is marked **Manually Adjusted**: re-saving the line refreshes the other months and leaves that one alone. **Re-split** (重新分解) on the line clears the marks and regenerates every month.
- Months that fall outside the range after a change are removed, unless manually adjusted.

Every total on the plan, the presales project's four Bizcase figures and the delivery project's Planned Total read this ledger, and the [Project Cost dashboard](/docs/analytics/dashboards) compares it with actuals by project, category and month.

## Versions, the baseline and adjustments

**Importing the Bizcase (step 27).** On a delivery project whose presales project is *approved*, **Import Bizcase Budget** clones the presales project's current Bizcase plan as delivery plan v1 — lines and manually adjusted months included — and writes the Bizcase total as the plan's **Frozen Baseline** and the project's Budget Baseline. The frozen baseline cannot be changed afterwards; a project that already has a plan refuses a second import.

**Editing (steps 28–31).** A plan's lines and months can be edited while the plan is a draft (or was sent back). Submit it with **Submit for Approval**; the finance lead approves it through the **成本计划审批** flow, and from then on its lines and months are frozen.

**Adjusting (step 32).** To change an approved plan, **New Plan Version** clones it into the next draft version. Adjust the lines there, then raise a [budget adjustment](/docs/projects/delivery-projects#budget-adjustments) that names the new version: its amount is read off the two versions (new total minus the version in force), and when the adjustment is approved the new version becomes the **current version** and the old one is marked *Superseded*. The project's Planned Total follows the current version only; a draft changes nothing until it is in force.

## What the approver sees

Approving a cost plan is a decision about a **difference**, not about a number in isolation: what matters is how this version compares with the version the project is executing on today. So submitting a plan records the version currently in force for the same project, copies its amounts onto the submitted plan, and shows each pair with the gap between them — the frozen baseline, the planned total and all four category totals including travel, each as *this version*, *the version in force* and *the difference*. The planned total also carries the gap as a percentage. A positive difference is an increase, a negative one a reduction; the differences are computed from the pair, so they can never disagree with the figures above them.

The snapshot is taken at the moment of submission — the same moment the plan's lines and months freeze — so the two sides of the comparison are exactly the two versions the approver is deciding between, and the record keeps them as the audit trail of what was put in front of them.

A project's **first** plan has nothing to compare against: the compared version is left empty and every amount reads as new spend, which is what a first version is.

**Cost Plans Awaiting Approval** is the same comparison as a queue — one row per plan in flight, every category's difference on the row — so an approver can rank a stack of them before opening any.

## Sharing

A cost plan is **private** to the cost manager who owns it; the sales manager and the system administrator read all. Its four line families and the month ledger are *Controlled by Parent*: whoever can see the plan can see, and while it is a draft edit, its lines. Travel standards, like rate cards, are shared master data.

## Tips for cost managers

- ✅ **Plan the Bizcase on the presales project first.** The presales project's Labor Cost, Third-Party Service Cost, Procurement Cost and Project Expense are the Bizcase plan's totals — there is nothing to type on the project itself.
- ✅ **Import once, then split.** The imported v1 already carries the Bizcase months; refine headcounts, months and unit prices on the lines and let the ledger follow.
- ✅ **Adjust by version, not by editing an approved plan.** A new version plus an approved adjustment keeps the frozen baseline and the audit trail intact.
